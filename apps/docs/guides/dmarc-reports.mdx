Mailbox providers like Google, Microsoft and Yahoo send a daily report to each domain that asks for one. It lists every server that sent mail using your domain, how many messages, and whether they passed SPF, DKIM and DMARC.

ByteSend reads those reports for you and shows:

- your DMARC **pass rate** over time,
- every **sending source**, with ByteSend's own traffic marked, so anything you don't recognise stands out,
- each **report** in full, as the provider sent it.

You need this before moving your DMARC policy from `p=none` to `quarantine` or `reject`: it tells you which legitimate services would start failing.

## Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the setup tab">
    Go to **DMARC → Setup**. Every verified domain is listed with its current DMARC record and your report address.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish the record">
    Copy the suggested record into a TXT record at `_dmarc.<your domain>`. If you already have a DMARC record, the suggestion keeps your policy and other report addresses, and only adds ours to `rua`. For example:

    ```
    _dmarc.acme.com  TXT  "v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:acme+dmarc@in.bytesend.cloud"
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait a day">
    Providers send reports about once every 24 hours. Once the setup tab shows **Reporting to ByteSend**, the first reports arrive within a day or two.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Your report address is your team's hosted address with `+dmarc`. If you [receive on a subdomain](/guides/inbound-email#receiving-on-your-own-domain) of the same domain, `dmarc@<that subdomain>` is used instead.
</Note>

## Reading the results

- **DMARC pass rate**: a message passes when SPF or DKIM passes *and* matches the domain in the From address. Aim for close to 100%.
- **Sending sources**: each server, by hostname where it has one. Sources marked **ByteSend** are your mail sent through us. For anything else:
  - if it's a service you use (your mailbox provider, a CRM, a helpdesk), set up SPF or DKIM for it with that provider;
  - if you don't recognise it and it fails, someone may be sending as your domain. A stricter policy (`p=quarantine`, then `p=reject`) tells providers to stop delivering it.
- **Reports**: open one to see each source with its DKIM signatures and SPF checks, and what the provider did with failing mail.

## Good to know

- Reports don't count toward your receiving allowance and don't appear in your inbox.
- Reports are kept for **90 days**.
- Only reports about your own domains are kept, and only from senders that pass SPF or DKIM, so nobody can fill your dashboard with fake data.
- Reports cover messages, not content: they never include subjects, recipients or bodies.

## Testing locally

With `pnpm dx:up` and `pnpm dev:local` running, open the emulator at `http://localhost:5350`, choose **Send a sample DMARC report**, and enter your report address and one of your domains.
